# Hive System Redesign — Proposal

A redesign of the AVP-Alien hive system that leans on BLib's faction *and*
territory systems, splits "hive" into two distinct concepts, and is built to
scale beyond what tickable-entity counts allow.

This document is design-only. No code changes are proposed yet — only data
shapes, lifecycles, and decisions to make. An implementation outline is at the
end.

---

## 1. Vocabulary

The word **hive** is overloaded in the current system. The redesign separates
it cleanly into a three-tier hierarchy:

```
Variant Faction (global, per variant)
   ├── eventually houses the Queen Mother (1 per variant per dimension)
   │
   └── Lineage Faction (per dimension, per bloodline)
          ├── empress (required once 2+ locations exist)
          │
          └── Hive Location (fixed point, owns territory + reserves + leader + bar)
                 ├── claimed chunks (BLib territory)
                 └── local leader (strongest xenomorph in territory)
```

| Term                  | Meaning                                                                          |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Variant Faction**   | A `Faction<VariantFactionData>` in BLib. Global across all dimensions, one per variant (NORMAL, ABERRANT, NETHER, IRRADIATED — at most 4 ever). Holds the **Queen Mother** for each dimension once that entity exists. Every alien of variant V is a member of variant V's faction. |
| **Lineage Faction**   | A `Faction<LineageFactionData>` in BLib. Per-dimension, per-bloodline. Created when a queen founds a new lineage. Holds members of that lineage. Has 0..N Hive Locations and 0 or 1 **Empress**. Variant-locked to its parent variant faction. |
| **Hive Location**     | A fixed `BlockPos` in a single dimension that anchors a contiguous claim of chunks. The *where*. Belongs to exactly one Lineage Faction. Owns local reserves, leadership, and a boss bar. |
| **Empress**           | The lineage-wide leader. Required to exist whenever a lineage owns 2+ locations. Without her, locations cannot dispatch reinforcement convoys to sister locations and cannot draw from the Lineage Pool for in-place spawns — each location is reduced to its local reserves only. |
| **Queen Mother**      | (Future entity, not yet implemented.) The variant-wide matriarch. Maximum one per variant per dimension. Lives at the variant-faction layer. Out of scope for v1, but the design has a slot for her. |
| **Lineage Pool**      | A per-lineage reserve of *shed* aliens. Fed by shedding (§4.4) and by abstract-convoy refunds when a destination dies mid-flight (§4.5.7). Drained only via empress-gated in-place reinforcement (§4.5.6) and lineage-zone spread spawns (§5.7). |
| **Variant Pool**      | A per-dimension, per-variant pool that catches lineage-pool overflow when a lineage dies. Reserved for future Queen Mother mechanics. |
| **Forager**           | An alien with no Lineage Faction membership (but still a Variant Faction member). A pre-state for joining or being shed. |
| **Biomass**           | The internal resource that powers territory expansion. Generated by hive activity, consumed by claims. Per-location. |

The everyday metaphor: think of a sci-fi galactic empire. The **species** is
the variant faction (every alien of variant V belongs). A **bloodline / royal
house** within that species is a lineage faction, founded by a queen and
governed by an empress when it spans multiple territories. A **city** is a
hive location, with a local king/queen and its own walls (claims). The species
endures across all bloodlines; bloodlines come and go; cities rise and fall.

## 2. Constraints satisfied

| # | Constraint                                                                  | Where it lives in this design                              |
|---|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Hive = faction + location, not one merged thing                             | §1, §3                                                     |
| 2 | Locations have a fixed center                                                | §3.3                                                       |
| 3 | Boss bar tied to location, distance-gated                                    | §6                                                         |
| 4 | Territory growth that gets more expensive but can be sustained               | §5 (biomass + claim cost curve)                            |
| 5 | Hive always has a leader (some kind of xenomorph)                            | §7                                                         |
| 6 | Numbers must scale beyond tickable-entity counts (reserves)                  | §4 (location-local + lineage pool)                         |
| 7 | Members shed to a global pool when they wander, not for young hives          | §4.4 (lineage pool, with maturity gates)                   |
| 8 | Variant-aware, mutually exclusive                                            | §3.1, §3.2 (variant lock at both faction tiers)            |
| 9 | Reserves localized to locations, not shared across distant locations         | §4.1, §4.5 (cross-location sharing is empress-gated)       |

Additional design constraints introduced in this revision:

| # | Constraint                                                                                                | Where it lives                                |
|---|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| A | One Variant Faction per variant, global across dimensions                                                  | §3.1                                          |
| B | One Lineage Faction per dimension per bloodline                                                            | §3.2                                          |
| C | Queens found new lineages if they don't already belong to one                                              | §8.2                                          |
| D | New locations may only be founded within range of an existing same-lineage location (lineages spread, not teleport) | §5.7                                  |
| E | A lineage with 2+ locations must have exactly one empress                                                  | §7.3, §8.4                                    |
| F | Without an empress, hive locations cannot draw from the Lineage Pool to reinforce other locations          | §4.5                                          |
| G | When the empress dies, the lineage shatters into N separate single-location lineages (civil war)           | §8.5                                          |

## 3. Data model

### 3.1 Variant Faction

A standard BLib `Faction<VariantFactionData>` keyed by a fixed
`ResourceLocation` per variant — there is exactly one per variant for the
life of the world:

```
avp_alien:variant/normal
avp_alien:variant/aberrant
avp_alien:variant/nether
avp_alien:variant/irradiated
```

These factions are **created lazily** the first time a member of that variant
is born/spawned, and are never deleted (persisting through periods where the
variant has no living members).

`VariantFactionData` fields:

```
VariantFactionData
  variant: AlienVariant                      // immutable
  queenMotherIdsByDimension: Map<ResourceKey<Level>, UUID>
                                             // FUTURE — populated when the Queen Mother
                                             // entity exists. Empty for v1.
  variantPoolsByDimension: Map<ResourceKey<Level>, EntityReserves>
                                             // overflow catchment from dying lineages
  ageInTicks: long
```

Membership: every alien of variant V is added to variant V's faction on its
first server-side load. Variant faction membership is **never** the basis for
spawn decisions or boss bars — it's a global registry that the Queen Mother
mechanic will hook into later, plus the dustbin for dead-lineage pool overflow.

The variant faction is always alive. Its `removalReason` is unused.

### 3.2 Lineage Faction

A standard BLib `Faction<LineageFactionData>` keyed by `avp_alien:lineage/<UUID>`.

Created when a queen founds a new bloodline (§8.2). Variant-locked at
creation and **must** match its parent variant faction's variant.

`LineageFactionData` fields:

```
LineageFactionData
  variant: AlienVariant                      // immutable, copy of parent variant faction's variant
  parentVariantFactionId: ResourceLocation   // back-pointer to the variant faction
  dimension: ResourceKey<Level>              // lineages are dimension-local
  locationIds: Set<HiveLocationId>           // owned hive locations
  empressId: UUID | null                     // null when 0 or 1 location, non-null when 2+
  founderId: UUID                            // queen that founded the lineage
  lineagePool: EntityReserves                // §4 — shed aliens accumulate here
  ageInTicks: long
  removalReason: LineageRemovalReason | null
```

`LineageRemovalReason` is a sealed interface (matches the project's stance
against enums for extensible types):

```java
sealed interface LineageRemovalReason {
    record NoLocationsRemain() implements LineageRemovalReason {}
    record AbsorbedBy(ResourceLocation absorberLineageId) implements LineageRemovalReason {}
    record CivilWar(Set<ResourceLocation> successorLineageIds) implements LineageRemovalReason {}
    record AdminRemoved(String reason) implements LineageRemovalReason {}
}
```

**Variant rule.** A lineage's variant is fixed at creation. An alien whose
variant differs from the lineage's variant cannot be a lineage member. If
an alien mutates mid-membership (e.g. becomes irradiated), the lineage's
`onMemberLoaded` detects the mismatch and ejects the alien to forager state.
The alien remains in *its* variant faction; it just leaves the lineage.

**Two same-variant lineages are independent.** Two queens of the same variant
in the same dimension found two separate lineages. They share a parent variant
faction (and the variant pool, indirectly), but their lineage pools, locations,
and empresses are distinct. They can fight each other, contest territory,
absorb each other (§8.8), or ignore each other.

### 3.3 Hive Location

A persisted, per-lineage structure:

```
HiveLocation
  id: HiveLocationId                    // typed wrapper around UUID
  lineageFactionId: ResourceLocation    // owning lineage faction
  dimension: ResourceKey<Level>         // copied from lineage; locations are dimension-local
  centerPos: BlockPos                   // FIXED — never moves after creation
  ageInTicks: long
  founderId: UUID                       // queen that founded this location
  leadershipManager: HiveLocationLeadership
  reserveManager: HiveLocationReserves
  bossBarManager: HiveLocationBossBar
  biomass: int                          // currency for expansion (§5)
  claimedChunks: Set<ChunkPos>          // mirror of BLib territory claims for THIS location
  removalReason: HiveLocationRemovalReason | null
```

**Center is fixed.** Set at creation, never updated. Solves the old system's
"hive shifts under players' feet" problem (`§9.3.11` of the analysis doc).
The founding queen (or the empress) can roam; the location stays put.

Hive locations are stored in a registry keyed by `HiveLocationId`. The
registry has dimension-keyed indexes (chunk-pos → location, and lineage-id →
locations) for O(1) lookups.

```
HiveLocationRegistry (server-global, like HiveRegistry today)
  byId:        Map<HiveLocationId, HiveLocation>
  byLineage:   Map<ResourceLocation, Set<HiveLocationId>>
  byChunk:     Map<ResourceKey<Level>, Map<ChunkPos, HiveLocationId>>
  byCenterDim: Map<ResourceKey<Level>, SpatialIndex<HiveLocation>>  // bucketed grid for nearest-by-pos lookups
```

The `byChunk` map is critical: it makes "which location owns this chunk"
O(1), which BLib's territory store can't tell us alone (BLib only knows
faction). It's persisted alongside the location records.

### 3.4 Lineage Pool

Held inside `LineageFactionData.lineagePool` as a single `EntityReserves`
keyed by `EntityType<?>`. Shedding (§4.4) increments it; reinforcement (§4.5)
and zone-spread spawning (§5.7) decrement it.

Why per-lineage rather than per-variant: a queen's bloodline is a real-world
collective; aliens shed from her hive's wandering should reinforce *her*
hives, not random same-variant hives across the map. This satisfies
constraint #9 (reserves are localized) at the right granularity — local
enough that distant lineages don't share, broad enough that aliens roaming
between sister hive locations of the same lineage are still useful.

### 3.5 Variant Pool (overflow / future Queen Mother)

Held inside `VariantFactionData.variantPoolsByDimension`, one
`EntityReserves` per dimension.

For v1, the variant pool is **only fed by lineage-pool overflow when a
lineage dies** (§8.7). It is reserved for future Queen Mother mechanics —
where she may, on emergence, spawn a horde drawn from this dimension-wide
pool of dead-lineage residue.

Until the Queen Mother exists as an entity, the variant pool is
write-only. It's persisted, it accumulates, and that's it. This is fine —
it does no harm and gives the future feature a population source.

### 3.6 Foragers

Foragers are aliens that:

- Are members of their **Variant Faction** (always — every alien is),
- Are *not* members of any Lineage Faction.

They carry no special data — their `HiveManager.lineage()` resolves to
`Option.none`. They're plain world entities.

A forager is created when:

- An alien is freshly spawned (e.g. natural spawn, spawn egg, hatch from an
  ovomorph that has no lineage attached), or
- An alien is shed from a lineage (§4.4), or
- A lineage dies (§8.7) and not all members were assigned to a successor.

A forager dies (or de-facto disappears) by:

- Being naturally despawned by the engine,
- Joining a same-variant lineage's hive location (entering its territory and
  staying for `FORAGER_JOIN_TICKS`, default 30 seconds).

## 4. Reserves

### 4.1 Local reserves (per location)

Each `HiveLocation` keeps an `EntityReserves` (BLib's existing multiset).
When a `HiveLocation` spawns members, it draws from its own reserves only,
**unless** an empress is present and the spawn is funded by the lineage
pool (§4.5).

This satisfies constraint #9 — distant same-lineage locations cannot
spontaneously transfer reserves; only empress-gated reinforcement transfers,
and only one entity at a time.

### 4.2 Sources of local reserves

| Source                                                           | Effect                                              |
|------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Aliens within the location's territory kill non-aliens           | +1 (or +genes-bonus) of caste's drone/runner/burster |
| Aliens within the location's territory despawn (chunk unload)    | +1 of own type — turning unloaded mass into reserves |
| Outer-edge xenomorphs idle for ≥ N seconds in the location's outer-ring chunks | +N drones/runners/bursters every M minutes (replaces the current "every 5 min" tick, scoped to the location) |
| Convoy arrival from a sister location (§4.5.4)                   | +N of caste types, drained from sister + transit losses |
| Empress-gated in-place pool reinforcement (§4.5.6)               | +1 of caste type, drained from lineage pool         |

### 4.3 Spawn gating

A location is allowed to spawn natural mob aliens only:

- Within its claimed chunks,
- When `reserves.canSpawn(entityType)` is true,
- And the position satisfies the per-caste *layer-by-distance* rule (§5.3).

Each successful spawn decrements the location's reserve count for that type.

This preserves the old system's "reserve-bounded spawning" behavior, just
local to a location.

### 4.4 Shedding (member → Lineage Pool)

An alien becomes a candidate for shedding when *all* of these are true:

- The alien is a member of a Lineage Faction,
- The alien is **outside** every claimed chunk of every location of its
  lineage continuously for at least `SHED_GRACE_TICKS` (default 5 minutes),
- The lineage has at least `MIN_LINEAGE_AGE_FOR_SHEDDING` total age
  (default 30 minutes — protects young lineages from bleeding members),
- The lineage owns at least 1 location,
- The alien is alive (not being killed; killed members are removed normally),
- The alien is **not** an empress. Empresses are exempt from shedding
  entirely — distance from territory has no effect on her lineage
  membership, and a player luring her to the far ends of the map
  does not break the lineage. The only thing that ends an empress's
  membership is her death (§8.5).
- The alien is **not** the local leader of any of its lineage's locations
  (the local leader stays affiliated until she's replaced or dies).

When all conditions are met:

1. The alien is removed from the lineage faction's membership.
2. The alien remains in the variant faction (its species membership is
   permanent for as long as it lives).
3. The lineage's `lineagePool` is incremented by 1 of the alien's entity type.
4. The alien entity is **despawned** (`Entity.discard()`). It is no longer
   a world entity. Its body is now an abstract count in the pool.

The despawn-on-shed is deliberate: it keeps the world's tickable-entity count
bounded while preserving the species' total population in the form of pool
counts. This is what enables population scaling beyond Minecraft's tick
limits (constraint #6).

### 4.5 Convoys — empress-gated, three types

Whenever the hive moves members across the world — to reinforce a sister,
to evacuate a dying location, or to retaliate against a player — it does
so via a **convoy**. A convoy is moving reserves: an abstract traveling
group that crosses the world over time (including through unloaded
chunks). When a player gets close enough to a convoy in transit, the
convoy *manifests* — its composition spawns as real entities in the
world that can fight or be fought. When the player walks away, the
manifested entities despawn and the convoy resumes abstract travel from
the centroid of where they were.

There are **three convoy types**, all built on the same machinery:

| Type           | Source                  | Destination               | Purpose                                                                |
|----------------|-------------------------|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Reinforcement  | Location A (with surplus)| Sister location B         | Move surplus members from a healthy location to a needy one           |
| Migration      | Location A (in danger)  | Sister location B         | Evacuate a dying location and fold its remnants into a sister         |
| Raid           | Location A (large enough)| Player position (moving)  | Strike back at a player who recently attacked the lineage             |

All three are **empress-gated** — without an empress alive, none can
dispatch.

#### 4.5.1 Common data model

```java
sealed interface Convoy {
    ConvoyId id();
    ResourceLocation lineageFactionId();
    ResourceKey<Level> dimension();
    Vec3 currentPos();                  // updated each tick
    EntityReserves composition();       // counts by EntityType — moving reserves
    float speedBlocksPerTick();
    long dispatchedTick();
    boolean manifested();
    Set<UUID> manifestedEntityIds();    // populated while manifested

    // §4.5.4
    record Reinforcement(/* common */ ...,
        HiveLocationId sourceLocationId,
        HiveLocationId destinationLocationId,
        BlockPos destinationPos
    ) implements Convoy {}

    // §4.5.5
    record Migration(/* common */ ...,
        HiveLocationId sourceLocationId,    // dies on dispatch (§4.5.5)
        HiveLocationId destinationLocationId,
        BlockPos destinationPos,
        boolean carriesEmpress              // true if empress was at source
    ) implements Convoy {}

    // §4.5.6
    record Raid(/* common */ ...,
        HiveLocationId sourceLocationId,
        UUID targetPlayerId,
        BlockPos lastKnownTargetPos,        // updates while target online + in dim
        long expiresAtTick
    ) implements Convoy {}
}
```

Convoys live on `LineageFactionData.convoys: List<Convoy>`. They're
sharded with the lineage faction's data, so they survive server
restarts.

#### 4.5.2 Common travel

Travel ticks regardless of chunk-load state. The lineage tick advances
every active convoy:

```
forEach convoy in lineage.convoys:
    target = computeTarget(convoy)              // type-specific
    direction = (target - convoy.currentPos).normalize()
    convoy.currentPos += direction * convoy.speedBlocksPerTick

    if shouldArriveOrTerminate(convoy):
        deliverOrDisband(convoy)                // type-specific
```

Type-specific target / arrival rules:

- **Reinforcement / Migration:** target is `destinationPos`. Arrives
  when `distance(currentPos, target) <= ARRIVAL_RADIUS_BLOCKS`.
- **Raid:** target is the target player's current position if they're
  online and in dimension; otherwise `lastKnownTargetPos`. Engages
  when within `RAID_ENGAGE_RADIUS_BLOCKS` AND target online + in dim.
  Terminates per §4.5.6 conditions.

Travel is **straight-line** through whatever's between source and
target. We do not pathfind through unloaded chunks. Convoys clip
through the world abstractly when nobody's looking; they only
interact with the world when manifested.

**Speed is composition-driven: a convoy moves at the speed of its
slowest unit.**

Each `EntityType<?>` has a per-type *speed contribution* in
blocks/tick. Drones and runners contribute high (they're fast on
their own); warriors and prowlers contribute moderate; praetorians,
crushers, queens, and empresses contribute low (they're heavy and
slow). Types without an explicit override fall back to the global
default `AlienProperties.Hive.CONVOY_SPEED_BLOCKS_PER_SECOND`
(default ~0.5 blocks/tick, ~10 blocks/sec).

The convoy's actual `speedBlocksPerTick` is computed as:

```
slowestContribution = min over all (type, count > 0) in composition:
    speedContributionFor(type)

speedBlocksPerTick = slowestContribution * convoyTypeMultiplier
```

Convoy-type multipliers compose on top:

- Reinforcement: 1.0× (default).
- Migration: 0.7× (the hive is slower when moving everything it owns).
- Raid: 1.5× (chasing, not marching).

So a runner-heavy raid convoy whose slowest member is a runner moves
much faster than a migration convoy whose slowest member is the
empress; a player who rolls warriors-only into a raid sees a faster
chase than a player who's about to be hit by a queen-led migration.

**Recomputation.** Speed is recomputed whenever the composition
changes — most importantly, when a manifested member dies and the
slowest type drops to zero count, the convoy *speeds up* for the
remainder of its journey. Players who whittle down the heavy units
of a manifested convoy and then disengage will find the abstract
remainder closing faster than they expected.

#### 4.5.3 Common manifestation

A convoy is **manifested** when *both*:

- `currentPos` is inside a chunk that is currently loaded by the
  server, AND
- At least one player is within `MANIFEST_DISTANCE_BLOCKS` (default
  80) of `currentPos`.

While manifested:

- Real `Entity` instances of each composition type are spawned at
  `currentPos` (clustered loosely, e.g. random offsets within ±3
  blocks).
- Entity UUIDs are recorded in `manifestedEntityIds`.
- Manifested entities are persistence-required while in transit, so
  they don't despawn on chunk unload while being tracked.
- Manifested entities are members of the lineage faction (and the
  variant faction).
- AI behavior is type-specific (§4.5.4–§4.5.6).
- If a manifested entity dies, the convoy's `composition` decrements
  by one of its type. **No refund** — those members are gone.
- If all manifested entities die AND composition is zero, the convoy
  is destroyed.

When manifestation conditions stop holding (no player in range OR
chunks unload):

- Manifested entities despawn.
- `currentPos` is updated from the entity centroid before despawn.
- The convoy continues abstractly with its remaining composition.

Convoys are therefore **moving reserves**. The composition is the
authoritative count; the manifested entities are a temporary
projection of those counts into the world while a player is around to
interact with them.

#### 4.5.4 Reinforcement convoys

Sister-to-sister rebalancing. The default convoy.

**Trigger.** Empress-gated and triggered by either:

- **Cry-for-help relay.** A `cryForHelp` event fires from one of B's
  xenomorphs and B's local reserves can't satisfy the summon. The
  empress checks all sister locations for surplus and picks the best
  donor (highest "donation score" = surplus over baseline + proximity
  to B + non-angry).
- **Empress directive.** A periodic lineage-tick task evaluates every
  pair `(A, B)` of the lineage's locations and dispatches a small
  convoy from any A whose local reserves significantly exceed its caps
  (i.e. surplus) toward any B whose local reserves are below their
  baseline. This balances the lineage over time.

**Dispatch effect.**
1. Drain a small composition from A's local reserves (typically 4–10
   members; configurable). Convoys are *not* funded from the lineage
   pool — they cost a sister's surplus.
2. Mint the convoy record and append to `LineageFactionData.convoys`.
3. Per-source cooldown: 5 minutes default between reinforcement
   dispatches from the same A.

**Manifestation behavior.** The "in transit" AI goal pulls members
toward `destinationPos` and resists permanent settling. They will
fight when attacked but won't seek players.

**Arrival.**
1. Drain composition into the destination's local reserves with
   overflow cascade (§4.6).
2. Despawn manifested entities if any.
3. Remove convoy record.

Arrival is not a spawn event — destination spawns members from those
new reserves over subsequent ticks via the normal spawn paths.

#### 4.5.5 Migration convoys

Whole-location evacuation. A dying location uproots and folds into a
sister.

**Trigger.** *Any* of the following AND the lineage has at least one
sister location AND the empress is alive:

- The location's queen has died and no living queen exists in the
  lineage that could resettle it within `RESETTLE_GRACE_TICKS`
  (default 5 minutes — gives the lineage time to send another queen).
- The location's biomass production has been below maintenance for
  `MIGRATION_BIOMASS_DECAY_TICKS` (default 30 minutes).
- The location's territory has shrunk to `≤ MIGRATION_TERRITORY_FLOOR`
  chunks (default 2).
- An admin command forces it.

**Destination selection.** The migration destination is the closest
sister location by straight-line distance from the source's center.
Ties are broken by largest claimed-chunk count (the strongest nearby
foothold). The destination is locked in at the moment trigger
conditions are met — even if a closer sister is founded mid-rally,
the convoy continues toward the originally selected destination.

**Dispatch effect.**

1. The source location enters an `Evacuating` state. Player-visible
   via the boss bar: color shifts to amber (`BossBarColor.YELLOW`)
   and the title is suffixed with " (Evacuating)". The shift is
   held through the full `MIGRATION_RALLY_TICKS` window. Used by
   AI to converge xenomorphs to a rally point near the center.
2. After `MIGRATION_RALLY_TICKS` (default 30 seconds — gives outlying
   members time to converge), the convoy dispatches:
   - **All** of the source's local reserves are drained into the
     convoy composition.
   - All living xenomorph members currently inside the source's
     territory are despawned and added to the composition.
   - If the empress is at the source location, she joins the convoy
     (`carriesEmpress = true`). She *manifests with the convoy* and
     is targetable while in transit.
   - Ovomorphs in the source's territory are **left behind** —
     they may hatch later, but the source location is decommissioned
     so they become orphaned. (Re-claiming those chunks later by an
     expansion of the destination is allowed.)
3. The source location is destroyed via the §8.6 path with these
   modifications:
   - The advancement on player-caused location death does **not**
     fire — the player didn't destroy this; the lineage abandoned
     it.
   - Any biomass remaining at the source is added to the convoy as
     a one-time payload (default cap +500), to be granted to the
     destination on arrival.
4. The destination location is flagged as the migration target. It
   may spawn additional xenomorphs to "make room" by spreading out
   members from its center, but this is optional (a quality-of-life
   knob, not a correctness requirement).

**Manifestation behavior.** "In transit" AI goal, same as
reinforcement — defensive, not aggressive. The empress, if present,
manifests with the convoy and uses normal queen/empress AI (still
defensive — she's fleeing, not raiding).

**Arrival.**
1. Drain composition into the destination's local reserves with
   overflow cascade.
2. Add the migration biomass payload to the destination's biomass.
3. If `carriesEmpress`, respawn the empress entity at the
   destination's center.
4. Despawn other manifested entities.
5. Remove convoy record.

**Sizing.** Migration convoys are typically **large** — they're not
a slice, they're the whole evacuation. Heavy spillover into the
lineage pool on arrival is expected and intentional.

**Failure to arrive.** If the migration convoy cannot complete (e.g.
destination dies, civil war, manifested-wipeout), the carried
empress (if any) becomes a forager — she retains her empress entity
type and lineage membership. If she survives long enough to settle,
she founds a new lineage location per §8.3 (potentially restoring the
lineage's footprint in a new place). If the rest of the composition
disbands, see §4.5.7.

#### 4.5.6 Raid convoys

Retaliatory strike against players who have attacked the lineage.

**Trigger.** Empress-gated and:

- A player has killed at least `RAID_THRESHOLD_KILLS` lineage members
  within the last `RAID_AGGRO_WINDOW_TICKS` (default 10 minutes), AND
  - Attribution defers to vanilla: a kill is credited to a player
    iff `LivingEntity.getKillCredit()` resolves to that `Player`
    (or `lastHurtByPlayer` is the player within Minecraft's
    standard attribution window). Indirect kills count whenever
    vanilla counts them — creeper aggro'd by the player, fall
    damage from a player-broken block, fire from a player-placed
    source, etc. We do not roll our own attribution logic.
- The lineage owns at least one location whose claimed-chunk count
  is `≥ RAID_MIN_LOCATION_SIZE` (default 8 chunks), AND
- That location has a per-source raid cooldown elapsed (default
  20 minutes between raids from the same source), AND
- That location does not already have an active raid convoy (one
  raid per source location at a time — see "Concurrency" below), AND
- The player is currently online (raids do not stalk offline players).

**Target selection.** When multiple players have aggro against the
lineage, the source picks the player with the **highest recent kill
count** against the lineage within the aggro window. Ties are broken
by most-recent kill timestamp.

**Concurrency.** A source location may have at most **one** active
raid convoy at any given time. Multiple source locations within the
same lineage may each dispatch their own raid concurrently — they
may converge on the same player (a coordinated assault from two
sides) or pursue different high-aggro players independently. The
empress does not coordinate target selection across sources; each
source picks its own target by the rule above. This may produce
emergent multi-front pressure on a player who has been busy across
the map, which is intended.

The "sufficiently large" threshold makes raids the prerogative of
established hives rather than every nascent 1-chunk location.

**Source.** The largest qualifying location of the lineage in the
same dimension as the target. Cross-dimension raids do not happen
in v1 — pursuing a player through a portal is out of scope.

**Destination.** The target player's *current position*, updated each
tick while they're online and in dimension. If they go offline or
change dimension, the convoy continues toward `lastKnownTargetPos`
and engages there if it reaches it.

**Composition.** Drawn from the source's local reserves, sized by
hive scale:

```
raidSize(source) =
    BASE_RAID_SIZE
  + RAID_SIZE_PER_CLAIMED_CHUNK * source.claimedChunks.size()
```

With `BASE_RAID_SIZE = 4` and `RAID_SIZE_PER_CLAIMED_CHUNK = 0.25`,
an 8-chunk source dispatches a 6-member raid; a 30-chunk source
dispatches an 11-member raid. Capped by source's actual reserve
counts. Composition skews toward warriors / runners / praetorians
rather than drones (a raid is fighters, not workers).

**Manifestation behavior.** **Aggressive.** The "engage target" AI
goal makes manifested members actively seek the target player. They
do not try to stay grouped — they break formation and attack. They
do not despawn just because the target leaves manifestation range;
the convoy's `currentPos` updates from the centroid as they chase.
A raid convoy can stay manifested for a long time as it engages.

**Termination conditions** (any):

- All manifested members die AND composition is 0 — the raid was
  repulsed; convoy is removed.
- The target player has been killed — raid succeeded; convoy
  disbands at `currentPos` with surviving members becoming
  foragers in the world (and surviving composition spilling to the
  lineage pool).
- `expiresAtTick` is reached (default 30 minutes after dispatch);
  convoy disbands in place per the disband-success path.
- The target player has been offline for
  `RAID_OFFLINE_TIMEOUT_TICKS` (default 5 minutes); the raid
  becomes a *return reinforcement* convoy back to source (or to
  the nearest sister if source is dead). This reuses the
  reinforcement record type — the raid record is replaced.
- The source location dies. The raid continues; on disband
  survivors return to the nearest sister or become foragers.

**Disband-success path.** Surviving manifested entities lose their
persistence-required flag and become standard forager-state world
entities (still members of the lineage faction unless they
subsequently shed). Abstract composition becomes lineage-pool
overflow.

**Disband-failure path** (raid wiped). Same as a manifested wipeout
on any other convoy: composition is zero, members are gone, convoy
is removed.

#### 4.5.7 In-place pool reinforcement (non-convoy, empress-gated)

The empress also enables a *non-convoy* path: a hive location may
spawn directly from the **lineage pool** when there's no sister
location to convoy from (single-location lineage), or when sister
convoys are unavailable (no surplus, on cooldown), or when the
needed response is too urgent for transit.

In-place pool reinforcement requires:

- Lineage has an alive empress, AND
- The location's local reserves can't satisfy the summon, AND
- The lineage pool has the requested type, AND
- The summon target is a vent inside the location's territory or a
  `cryForHelp` event from a member of that location.

Effect: lineage pool ↓ 1 → spawn at vent (no convoy travel). This
preserves the old "vent reinforcement" behavior for situations where
no convoy makes sense.

**Why both paths exist.** Convoy reinforcement (location → location)
costs from a sister's surplus and is interceptable. In-place pool
reinforcement (pool → location) costs from the abstract pool of shed
members and is uninterceptable. Convoys are the *normal* path when
sister locations exist; pool spawn is the fallback.

Without an empress, **neither** path is available — locations are
fully reduced to their own local reserves.

#### 4.5.8 Failure modes (across all convoy types)

| Scenario                                                  | Behavior                                                                                       |
|-----------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Destination location dies mid-flight (Reinforcement / Migration) | Convoy disbands at `currentPos`. If manifested, entities become foragers. If abstract, composition spills to the lineage pool. For Migration, if `carriesEmpress`, she becomes a forager at `currentPos` and may found a new location later. |
| Source location dies mid-flight                            | Reinforcement: continues; `sourceLocationId` set to null. Migration: source is already destroyed at dispatch — N/A. Raid: continues; on disband, survivors route to nearest sister or become foragers. |
| Empress dies mid-flight                                    | All in-flight convoys continue (already committed). No new convoys can be dispatched until a new empress emerges. If civil war happens (§8.5), see next row. |
| Civil war happens mid-flight                               | Active convoys disband at `currentPos`. Composition is partitioned per the civil-war rules: each member is reassigned to the new lineage whose successor location is closest to `currentPos`, and added to that lineage's pool. Manifested entities become foragers. Migration's empress (if `carriesEmpress`) becomes a forager. |
| Lineage absorbed into another (§8.8) mid-flight            | Convoy is reassigned to the absorbing lineage. Source/destination ids are remapped if their locations transferred; if they no longer exist, the convoy disbands per the destination-died rule. |
| Destination location's reserve caps are full on arrival    | Overflow spills via §4.6 cascade (location → lineage pool → variant pool). For Migration, this is normal — migrations are oversized.|
| Manifested convoy is fully wiped by players                | Composition is zero; convoy is removed. Members are *lost* — neither pool nor reserves get them back. (This is the only way reinforcement strength is permanently destroyed in the system.) |
| Server stops mid-flight                                    | Convoy persists with the lineage's faction data; on next start it resumes from `currentPos`.  |
| Raid target player goes offline / changes dimension        | Raid converts to a return reinforcement to source or nearest sister (§4.5.6).                  |
| Raid target player is killed                               | Raid succeeds; convoy disbands at `currentPos`. Manifested survivors become foragers; abstract composition spills to the lineage pool. |

The asymmetry persists: **abstract convoy losses (destination died,
expired, etc.) refund to the pool; manifested combat losses do not.**
This is intentional — players who intercept a convoy in person are
doing real damage.

### 4.6 Soft caps and overflow

Local-reserve and lineage-pool entries have per-type soft caps tied to size:

```
maxLocalReservesForType(loc, type) =
    BASE_LOCAL_CAP_FOR_TYPE
  + LOCAL_CAP_PER_CLAIMED_CHUNK_FOR_TYPE * loc.claimedChunks.size()

maxLineagePoolForType(lineage, type) =
    BASE_LINEAGE_CAP_FOR_TYPE
  + LINEAGE_CAP_PER_LOCATION_FOR_TYPE * lineage.locationIds.size()
```

Spillover rules:

- A source that would push **local reserves** over the cap spills the
  excess to the **lineage pool**.
- A source that would push **lineage pool** over the cap spills the excess
  to the **variant pool** for that dimension.
- The variant pool is uncapped (or has a very high cap) — the buck stops
  here.

This three-tier spillover means the species never "loses" population — it
just propagates upward through the hierarchy until it lands somewhere with
capacity, and is available for the right-level mechanism (location spawning,
empress-gated cross-location reinforcement, future Queen Mother spawning).

## 5. Territory & growth

### 5.1 The expansion loop

The hive grows by claiming new chunks via BLib's `TerritoryManager`. The
mod-side `HiveLocation` records the claim under its `claimedChunks` set
and updates the `byChunk` index. BLib's territory claim is filed under the
**lineage** faction id (since BLib only knows about factions, not locations).

A `HiveLocation` may claim a `ChunkPos` only if:

1. **Adjacency.** The chunk is cardinally adjacent to a chunk already
   claimed by *this same location* (mod's `byChunk` index — not BLib's
   per-faction view, which would conflate sister locations).
2. **No same-lineage overlap.** The chunk is not already claimed by a
   different location of *the same lineage* (per `byChunk`).
3. **No same-variant overlap by another lineage.** Different lineages of
   the same variant *can* contest, but cross-variant overlap is not
   allowed (BLib's claim model permits it; we forbid it at the AVP layer).
4. **Biomass cost paid.** The location has enough biomass to spend (§5.4).
5. **Cooldown elapsed.** A per-location claim cooldown has elapsed
   (default 30 seconds) to keep growth from happening in bursts and to
   give the server room to breathe.
6. **Activity gate.** A xenomorph member of the location stood in the
   target chunk within the last `CLAIM_ACTIVITY_WINDOW` ticks. *No
   activity, no claim.* This forces growth to be earned — the hive
   spreads where its members have actually been.

When all are met, the location:

- Spends biomass,
- Calls `Alien.MOD.territories().addClaim(level, pos, lineageFactionId)`,
- Adds the chunk to its `claimedChunks`,
- Updates `byChunk` index.

### 5.2 Contested chunks

If a different-variant location's territory borders the target chunk, both
locations may claim it simultaneously. BLib supports multiple claimants —
`isContested` returns true. While contested, neither side's normal claim
benefits apply; the chunk ticks faster on the location whose members
actually occupy it (resolution rule below).

Same-variant cross-lineage contesting works the same way: two lineages of
variant ABERRANT in the same dimension can fight over a chunk.

**Contest resolution.** Every `CONTEST_TICK_WINDOW` (default 60 seconds),
each contested chunk evaluates: which contesting location has more
xenomorph members within the chunk? The losing location's claim is
*removed* from BLib for that chunk. If it was the only claim still
contesting, the winner becomes the sole claimant.

### 5.3 Caste distance rule

The old system's percentile-based sphere layers are replaced with a
straight-line distance from the location's center, expressed in chunks:

| Caste                      | Allowed distance (chunks from center) |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Queen / Empress            | 0 (center chunk only)                |
| Praetorian / Crusher       | 0–2                                  |
| Drone / Runner / Ovomorph  | 0–6                                  |
| Warrior / Prowler          | 2–10                                 |

These thresholds are config (`AlienProperties.Hive.*_RANGE_CHUNKS`).
Crucially, distance is to the *fixed center* — moving the hive is no
longer a thing. The empress, when present, lives at the center of one
location (§7.3.2).

A spawn is only valid in a chunk that:
- The location *claims*,
- And whose distance from `centerPos.chunkPos` matches the caste's
  allowed range.

This gives shape (warriors patrol the edge, queens near the center)
without the old fragile percentile sphere.

### 5.4 Biomass — the growth resource

Biomass is the currency a location spends to claim chunks. It's
generated by hive activity inside claimed chunks, so a healthy hive
*can keep growing*; a starved hive *cannot*.

Sources of biomass (per location, per second):

| Source                                                                  | Biomass             |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------|
| Each loaded xenomorph standing in a claimed chunk                        | +1                  |
| Each kill of a non-alien mob inside claimed chunks                       | +25                 |
| Each successful resin block placed in a claimed chunk by a member       | +5                  |
| Each ovomorph existing inside claimed chunks                             | +0.5                |
| Empress present at this location (passive)                               | +5                  |
| Idle bonus (location is non-angry and chunk-loaded)                      | +N flat (small)     |

Biomass is consumed only by claim attempts:

```
claimCost(loc, targetChunk) =
    BASE_CHUNK_COST
  * (1 + loc.claimedChunks.size() * GROWTH_FACTOR)
```

With `BASE_CHUNK_COST = 100` and `GROWTH_FACTOR = 0.05`, the first
chunk costs 100, the tenth costs 150, the fiftieth costs 350, the
hundredth costs 600. The cost is super-linear but not exponential —
the hive *can* outpace it as long as its membership keeps producing
biomass roughly proportionally to its size.

This satisfies constraint #4: growth gets more expensive, but if the
hive's biomass production scales with its membership (and membership
scales with territory because the caste rule lets bigger territories
host more aliens), the hive keeps up.

### 5.5 Territory shrinkage

Territory shrinks when:

- The location's biomass production is below maintenance for `T`
  consecutive minutes — claims at the *farthest* radius are released
  one per minute. ("The hive can't sustain its borders.")
- A contested chunk loses its contest (§5.2).
- An admin command forces it.
- The location dies (§8.6).

The "release outermost first" rule maintains the visual shape of the
hive contracting toward its queen.

### 5.6 No more "leash sphere"

Membership is no longer distance-gated by a sphere. An alien is a member
of a lineage until it's *killed* or *shed* (§4.4). Aliens may freely roam
inside their lineage's territory across multiple locations.

### 5.7 Lineage spread distance

This is the mechanism by which a lineage geographically expands. New hive
locations can only be founded by queens of the lineage, and only within a
spread-zone of existing same-lineage locations.

Specifically: a queen Q of lineage L attempting to settle at position P
may found a new location only if **both**:

- `P.dimension == L.dimension`, AND
- There exists at least one location L' in L such that
  `chunkDistance(P, L'.centerPos) <= MAX_LINEAGE_SPREAD_CHUNKS`
  (default 32 chunks, configurable).

If P is outside the spread zone of every existing same-lineage location,
the queen cannot extend the lineage there. Two outcomes are possible
depending on the queen's lineage state at that moment:

- **Queen is currently in the lineage:** the queen is rejected at P; she
  must wait, retry settling closer, or be shed (§4.4) if she stays away
  long enough. Shedding her removes her from the lineage; the next time
  she attempts settlement she'll fall into the next bullet.
- **Queen is a forager (no lineage):** she founds a *new* lineage at P
  (§8.2).

The spread-zone constraint is the geographic backbone of the lineage
concept — a lineage doesn't randomly teleport across the world; it
expands by gradually dropping new hive locations within reach of its
existing ones, as shed members drift outward.

How shed members enable spread (the loop):

1. A xenomorph in lineage L wanders away from L's territory and is shed
   (§4.4) — it joins L's lineage pool and despawns.
2. Some time later, an in-zone spawn event (e.g. natural spawn check, or
   an explicit "lineage spread" task on the lineage faction) draws from
   the lineage pool to spawn a new alien within the lineage's spread zone
   but **outside** any existing location's territory (i.e., into the
   periphery).
3. If the spawned alien is a queen and she finds a viable settlement spot
   that's within the spread-zone but outside any current claimed
   territory, she settles per §8.3 and a new location of L is created.

The result: lineages grow outward through a slow, organic process driven
by shedding and zone spawns. They never appear out of thin air across the
map. They cannot leapfrog beyond `MAX_LINEAGE_SPREAD_CHUNKS` of existing
holdings.

If a lineage has 2+ locations spaced near the maximum spread distance,
its effective spread reach is the union of zones around each location.
This means a long, stretched lineage can extend further across the map
than a balled-up one — at the cost of being more spread-thin (worse
contest performance, harder to defend).

## 6. Boss bar

One `ServerBossEvent` per `HiveLocation`. Constraint #3 explicitly:
the bar is the *location's* bar.

### 6.1 Visibility (player → bar attachment)

A player is added to a location's boss bar when:

1. The player is in the same dimension as the location, AND
2. The player is within `BOSS_BAR_DISPLAY_RADIUS_BLOCKS` (default 96
   blocks) of the location's center, AND
3. The player is a valid target for the location's variant
   (`AlienPredicates.isValidTarget(variant, player)`).

This is purely *distance from center* — no need to be inside claimed
chunks. A player approaching the location sees the bar before they
hit any aliens, which gives a clear "you are entering hive territory"
moment.

### 6.2 Removal

A player is removed when:

- They leave the radius,
- They change dimension,
- They become an invalid target,
- The location dies.

Re-evaluation runs every 20 ticks (matches today's cadence).

### 6.3 Progress (xenomorph count)

The progress is *relative to the location only*:

```
loaded = location.lineage.getLoadedMembersByType()
  filter (uuid -> location.byChunk contains entity's current chunk)

reserves = location.localReserves.getCountMatching(XENOMORPHS)

progress = (loaded.count(XENOMORPHS) + reserves) / location.peakXenomorphCount
```

**Important:** `loaded.count` here filters to entities physically inside
this location's claimed chunks. An alien that's a member of the
lineage but currently in another location's territory is *not* counted
on this bar. This satisfies the constraint that the boss bar is
"relative" to the location, even when sister locations of the same
lineage are nearby.

The lineage pool count is **not** added to the progress — the bar
represents what the location can immediately bring to bear locally,
not the broader lineage's reserves.

`peakXenomorphCount` decays slowly (1 per minute) so a softened-up
hive that recovers eventually shows a full bar again. (Fixes the old
system's `maximumSeenXenomorphCount` only-grows bug.)

### 6.4 "Angry" state

Same idea as today: a location is `isAngry()` if its bar has at least
one player. Angry locations:

- Don't claim new chunks (claim attempts pause),
- Don't release outermost chunks (no shrinkage during a raid),
- Don't shed members (a player attack shouldn't accelerate shedding),
- Aliens within the location are persistence-required.

When the last player leaves the radius and `ANGRY_GRACE_TICKS`
elapses, the location returns to peaceful state.

### 6.5 "Evacuating" state

When a location enters the `Evacuating` state during a migration
dispatch (§4.5.5), the boss bar gives players an explicit visual
cue:

- Color shifts to amber (`BossBarColor.YELLOW`), overriding the
  variant's normal color for the duration.
- Title is suffixed with " (Evacuating)" — e.g. "Aberrant Hive
  (Evacuating)".
- Held for the full `MIGRATION_RALLY_TICKS` window (default 30
  seconds), then resolves when the migration convoy departs and the
  location dies (the bar is dismissed entirely on location death,
  per §6.2).

This is the player's signal that their pressure broke the hive's
will to hold the location, and it gives them a visible window in
which to disrupt the rally — every xenomorph killed during the
30-second rally never makes it into the migration convoy's
composition (per §4.5.5 step 2: composition is drained at the
*end* of the rally window).

## 7. Leadership

The leadership concept now exists at three layers:

| Layer            | Role                                              | Selection rule                                                                |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Location         | Local leader (constraint #5)                       | Highest-disposition xenomorph physically inside the location's claimed chunks |
| Lineage          | Empress (constraint E)                             | A queen who has been promoted; required when the lineage owns 2+ locations    |
| Variant          | Queen Mother (future)                              | TBD — out of scope for v1, but the slot exists in `VariantFactionData`        |

### 7.1 Per-location leadership

Every `HiveLocation` runs `PickBestLeaderTask` independently. The
disposition table from `HiveLeaderDispositionUtil` is reused
(Queen=4 … Drone=0). Leader is the highest-disposition loaded
xenomorph **inside the location's claimed chunks**.

**Crucial change from the old system:** the leader lookup is restricted
to xenomorphs *physically present* in the location's territory. This
prevents the queen of location A from being miscounted as the leader
of location B just because both belong to the same lineage.

If the empress is physically present at a location, she counts as a
leader candidate for that location and will outrank a normal queen
(empress disposition is 4.5 or simply outranks queen via a
sub-comparison).

### 7.2 Vacancy

If no xenomorph is in the location's territory, the location enters a
**Dormant** state:

- No claim attempts,
- No spawning (reserves are frozen, not lost),
- No leader,
- Boss bar progress reads zero,
- The location is *not dead* — eggs and facehuggers in the territory
  keep the location alive (see §8.6).

When a xenomorph re-enters the territory, the location wakes up,
PickBestLeader runs on its next cadence, and normal operation
resumes.

This means the constraint "a hive has a leader at all times" is
relaxed slightly: a *living* location has a leader whenever it has
any xenomorphs. A dormant location is "in stasis" — not failing the
constraint, but waiting.

### 7.3 Empress

The empress is the lineage-wide leader, required to exist whenever the
lineage owns 2+ hive locations.

#### 7.3.1 Mandatory presence

The invariant is:

```
lineage.locationIds.size() >= 2  =>  lineage.empressId != null && empress is alive
```

The lineage faction's tick checks this invariant and triggers empress
emergence (§8.4) if it's broken.

#### 7.3.2 Where she lives

The empress is anchored to one specific hive location at any moment —
called her **seat**. The seat is the location where she physically
resides. She may travel between locations, but at any tick exactly one
of the lineage's locations is her seat (the location her current
chunk-pos belongs to, or the last seat if she's currently in transit
through unclaimed territory).

She does *not* have to be in any specific location's center — she
roams within and between them. She is, however, persistence-required
across all chunks of all of her lineage's locations (special-case
beyond normal leader persistence).

**Wandering far from territory.** A player can lure the empress
arbitrarily far from any of her lineage's locations. This is allowed
and has *no* gameplay consequences for the lineage's structure: she
is exempt from shedding (§4.4), her seat continues to be her last
in-territory location, and convoy dispatch / in-place pool
reinforcement (§4.5) remain available at any of the lineage's
locations as long as she is alive. The lineage does not break
because the empress is far away — only because she dies (§8.5). A
player who leads her thousands of blocks into the wilderness is
making her vulnerable but is not, by that act alone, weakening the
lineage's coordination.

#### 7.3.3 What she enables

Her presence (alive, anywhere in the world) enables:

- **All three convoy types.** Reinforcement, Migration, and Raid
  convoys (§4.5.4–§4.5.6) are all empress-gated. Without her, no
  convoys can be dispatched at all — the lineage cannot rebalance,
  cannot evacuate dying locations, and cannot retaliate against
  players.
- **In-place pool reinforcement.** Hive locations can pull from the
  lineage pool to spawn reinforcements at vents/cry-for-help events
  (§4.5.7). Without her, only local reserves are usable.
- **Lineage spread spawns.** The lineage-spread loop (§5.7) requires
  an empress before it will spawn shed members back into the world.
  Without her, shed members stay in the pool indefinitely (they don't
  decay; they're just inert).
- **Faction-wide broadcasts.** A `cryForHelp` event from one location
  can summon reinforcements at *that* location funded by the lineage
  pool. Without her, cries-for-help are limited to local reserves.

#### 7.3.4 What she does NOT do

- She does not change per-location leadership; each location still picks
  its own local leader. The empress is at most a candidate for being a
  location's local leader if she's physically there.
- She does not change membership rules; aliens are still members of the
  lineage based on the normal join/shed rules.
- She does not move location centers (centers are fixed forever).

#### 7.3.5 Disposition and leadership comparisons

For purposes of `PickBestLeaderTask`, the empress outranks a queen when
both are present in the same location's territory. The disposition
table extends:

| Rank | Type                                  |
|------|---------------------------------------|
| 5    | Empress                               |
| 4    | Queen                                 |
| 3    | Predalien                             |
| 2    | Praetorian / Crusher                  |
| 1    | Warrior / Prowler                     |
| 0    | Drone / Runner                        |
| -1   | (anything else)                       |

#### 7.3.6 Sole-empress invariant

There is at most one empress per lineage. Attempts to create a second
empress (e.g. via spawn egg, command, or some game-event) are blocked
at the entity-creation layer. If a player somehow ends up with two
empress entities for the same lineage, the lineage-tick auto-resolves
by picking the older one as canonical and converting the other(s) back
to queen entities (or simply removing them — implementation choice).

### 7.4 Queen Mother (future)

Reserved for a future entity. Shape:

- One per variant per dimension (so up to 4 per dimension).
- Lives at the variant-faction layer.
- Tracked in `VariantFactionData.queenMotherIdsByDimension`.
- Likely role: spawns from the variant pool, oversees all lineages of
  her variant in her dimension, has world-shaking abilities, etc.

For v1, the Queen Mother does not exist. The variant faction's
`queenMotherIdsByDimension` is an empty map. The variant pool
accumulates pool overflow for her future arrival.

## 8. Lifecycles

### 8.1 Variant Faction creation

A variant faction is created **lazily** the first time an alien of variant
V is server-side loaded with no existing variant-V faction. This is a
near-server-startup event — typically variant factions are created within
the first few minutes of a fresh world's first session, then persist
forever.

The faction's id is fixed (`avp_alien:variant/<variant_name>`), so creation
is idempotent: subsequent calls just return the existing faction.

There is no "variant faction death" path. Variant factions persist for the
life of the world even when their member count is zero.

### 8.2 Lineage Faction creation

A new `LineageFaction` is created when a queen of variant V settles at a
position P that meets *all* of:

- The queen is currently a forager (no existing lineage), OR she is a
  member of a lineage L but P is outside L's spread zone (§5.7) AND she
  has been outside L's territory long enough to have been shed (in which
  case she's already a forager when this check runs), AND
- No existing lineage's spread zone covers P (so this is a "fresh ground"
  settlement), AND
- A "settlement validity" check passes (e.g. solid ground, near a
  resin-replaceable surface, optional Y-level constraint), AND
- The queen has stood in P's chunk for `SETTLEMENT_TICKS` (default 60
  seconds) without combat (constraint: settling is deliberate, not a
  side effect of spawning).

Effect:

1. Look up (or lazily create) the variant faction for V (§8.1).
2. Mint a fresh `ResourceLocation` for the lineage faction.
3. Create the BLib lineage faction with `LineageFactionData`.
4. Set `parentVariantFactionId`, `dimension`, `founderId = queen.uuid`.
5. Add the queen as a member of the lineage.
6. Found the lineage's first hive location at P (§8.3).
7. (No empress yet — only one location.)

This is the only path by which lineages come into existence. Non-queen
xenomorphs cannot create lineages.

### 8.3 Hive Location creation

A new `HiveLocation` is created when:

- A queen of lineage L wants to settle at position P, AND
- P is within L's spread zone (or this is L's founding location, in
  which case the spread-zone check is bypassed), AND
- P is outside every existing location of any same-variant lineage's
  territory (no two same-variant locations on the same chunks), AND
- The same "settlement validity" check from §8.2 passes, AND
- The queen has stood at P for `SETTLEMENT_TICKS`.

Effect:

1. Mint a `HiveLocationId`.
2. Set `centerPos` to the queen's chunk center.
3. Initial claim: the center chunk only, claimed by L's faction id.
4. Insert into `HiveLocationRegistry`'s three indexes.
5. Initial reserves: a small bootstrap budget (1 drone, 1 runner).
6. Initial biomass: 0.
7. Add the location id to `LineageFactionData.locationIds`.
8. **If this brings the lineage to 2+ locations**, trigger empress
   emergence (§8.4).

### 8.4 Empress emergence

When a lineage transitions from 1 location to 2 locations, an empress
must be created.

Selection of the empress candidate is **deterministic** (no combat
ritual in v1 — see §14 #11). Among all queens currently members of
the lineage, pick the highest-priority candidate by these comparators
in order:

1. **Older queens preferred** (more battle-tested) — compare
   `entity.tickCount`.
2. **Queens at the older location preferred** (the lineage's
   "capital") — compare the queen's seat location's `ageInTicks`.
3. **Founder tiebreak** — if the lineage's `founderId` matches one
   of the still-tied queens, that queen wins.
4. **UUID tiebreak** — lexicographic compare of UUIDs as a
   guaranteed total order, so the selection is fully deterministic
   and reproducible across server restarts even in pathological
   tie scenarios.

The chosen queen is **promoted** to empress through a visible
30-second molt-style ritual:

1. The queen enters the molt state. She becomes **invulnerable**
   for the molt's duration (30 seconds — death during molt would
   invalidate emergence and is disallowed), **immobile** (animation
   overrides movement), and **persistence-required** (chunk unload
   is suppressed). A molt animation plays — reuse the existing
   `MoltingManager` visual pipeline where it fits.
2. At molt end, her queen entity is discarded and replaced with
   the appropriate variant's empress entity at her position
   (`EMPRESS`, `ABERRANT_EMPRESS`, `NETHER_EMPRESS`, or
   `IRRADIATED_EMPRESS`). Health, hive membership, and lineage
   data transfer. Set `lineage.empressId = empress.uuid`.

This gives players a clear, telegraphed event — "a queen is
becoming an empress" — rather than an instantaneous swap that
would leave them confused about the new entity that just appeared.

If no queen is currently alive in the lineage when this trigger fires
(unusual — would require all queens dead between location founding and
location-2 creation), the empress emergence stalls and an alert is
logged. The lineage tick will retry the emergence on every subsequent
tick until a queen is available.

The empress entity already exists in the codebase
(`com.alien.common.gameplay.entity.living.alien.xenomorph.empress.Empress`),
registered as `EMPRESS`, `ABERRANT_EMPRESS`, `NETHER_EMPRESS`, and
`IRRADIATED_EMPRESS` in `AlienEntityTypes` — one per variant — and
already tagged under `AlienEntityTypeTags.EMPRESSES`. Promotion at
emergence time uses the appropriate empress entity type for the
queen's variant (a normal queen becomes an `EMPRESS`, an aberrant
queen becomes an `ABERRANT_EMPRESS`, etc.).

### 8.5 Empress death and civil war

When the empress of a multi-location lineage dies, the lineage shatters.

Trigger: `Empress.remove(KILLED)` or any other death pathway. (Despawn
via chunk unload doesn't count — the empress is persistence-required
across all of her lineage's territory.)

Civil war procedure:

1. Snapshot the lineage's current state: locations, members,
   lineage-pool contents.
2. For each existing location of the lineage, mint a **new** lineage
   faction (one per location). Each new lineage:
   - Inherits the dead lineage's variant.
   - Has `dimension` = the dead lineage's dimension.
   - `parentVariantFactionId` = the same variant faction.
   - `founderId` = the local leader of that location at the moment of
     civil war (or null if the location is dormant).
   - Has exactly one location id in `locationIds` — the location it
     inherited.
   - `empressId = null` (single-location lineages don't need one).
   - Starts with a share of the dead lineage's pool: split *equally*
     across the new lineages, with any remainder going to the lineage
     of the oldest location.
3. Members are migrated:
   - For each living member of the dead lineage:
     - If the member is currently inside a claimed chunk of one of the
       dead lineage's old locations, they join that location's new
       lineage.
     - Otherwise, they become foragers (no lineage). They may re-attach
       to any same-variant lineage by entering its territory later
       (§3.6).
4. The dead lineage faction is removed from BLib (`Alien.MOD.factions().remove`).
   Its `removalReason` is `CivilWar(successorLineageIds)` — recorded in
   logs for forensics.
5. Players within the boss-bar radius of any location of the lineage
   receive a "Lineage Shattered" advancement (separate from
   `KILL_A_HIVE_LOCATION` — see §8.6).

After civil war, the new lineages may behave like any same-variant
lineages: they may contest each other's territory, they may absorb each
other (§8.8), or they may quietly ignore each other and grow
independently. The civil war does NOT make them hostile-by-default —
their relationship is governed by the same rules as any same-variant
lineage pair.

### 8.6 Hive Location death

A location dies when *all* of:

- It has zero claimed chunks (territory fully released), OR
- It is dormant (no xenomorphs in territory) AND has zero ovomorphs
  in territory AND has been dormant ≥ `LOCATION_DECAY_TICKS`
  (default 24 hours of game time).

On death:

- All remaining members in the location's territory are released to
  forager state (NOT killed) — they may re-attach to other
  same-variant locations.
- BLib claims for this location are released.
- The location's local reserves spill to the lineage pool (capped per
  §4.6; further overflow goes to the variant pool).
- The location is removed from the registry.
- Players within the boss-bar radius receive
  `DESTROY_A_HIVE_LOCATION` advancement, but only when the death is
  due to player action ("killed"), not natural decay.
- The location id is removed from `LineageFactionData.locationIds`.
- **If the lineage now has exactly 1 location and an empress exists,
  the empress is *not* automatically demoted** — she remains empress.
  The 1-location-empress state is allowed (it's only the 0-location
  state that triggers further lifecycle change). This means a lineage
  that lost 3 of its 4 locations still has an empress; she's the
  insurance policy in case the lineage rebuilds.
- **If the lineage now has 0 locations**, see §8.7.

### 8.7 Lineage Faction death

A lineage dies when *both* of:

- It has zero locations, AND
- The condition has held for `LINEAGE_DECAY_TICKS` (default 1 hour).

(A lineage can have living members and zero locations transiently —
e.g. all locations were destroyed in quick succession. The
`LINEAGE_DECAY_TICKS` grace period gives surviving members time to
re-found a location before the lineage formally dies.)

On death:

- The empress (if any) is released to forager state. She no longer has
  a lineage; she may found a new one by settling.
- All living members are released to forager state.
- The lineage pool spills to the dimension's variant pool (capped per
  §4.6).
- BLib `Alien.MOD.factions().remove(lineageId)` is called.
- BLib `BLibFactionRemoveEvent` fires; AVP-Alien's listener cleans up
  any stragglers.

### 8.8 Lineage absorption

Two same-variant lineages whose territories become **cardinally
adjacent** (a chunk of one is cardinally adjacent to a chunk of the
other, per the mod's `byChunk` index) merge after a strict cooldown.
This is the consolidation path for AI-driven lineages that grow into
each other — without it, two mid-size lineages that meet would burn
forever in border skirmishes instead of coalescing.

#### 8.8.1 Eligibility

Both lineages must satisfy *all* of:

- Same variant.
- Same dimension.
- At least one chunk of one is cardinally adjacent to at least one
  chunk of the other (using each lineage's `byChunk` index — not the
  BLib per-faction view, which is irrelevant when both are still
  separate factions).
- Both lineages are **non-angry** (no players currently engaging
  any of either lineage's locations via boss bar).
- Territories have been adjacent **continuously for ≥5 minutes**
  (prevents drive-by absorptions when a wandering convoy or scout
  briefly ticks adjacency).

When all hold, the merge fires.

#### 8.8.2 Strength comparison

The **stronger** lineage absorbs the **weaker** one. Strength is
compared in order:

1. Total living xenomorph member count.
2. Empress age — older empress wins (compare `entity.tickCount`).
3. Founder queen age — older founder wins.
4. Lineage faction `ageInTicks` — older lineage wins.
5. Lexicographic compare of lineage faction `ResourceLocation` —
   guarantees a total order so the result is reproducible.

#### 8.8.3 Transfer

When the merge fires:

- **Locations.** Every location of the weaker lineage is reassigned
  to the stronger's faction id. For every chunk in each transferred
  location's `claimedChunks`, BLib `transferClaim(level, pos, weakerId,
  strongerId)` is called. The mod's `byLineage` and `byChunk` indexes
  are updated. Each transferred location's `lineageFactionId` is
  rewritten.
- **Members.** Every living member of the weaker lineage is added to
  the stronger's faction membership. Variant faction membership is
  unchanged (members were already members of the same variant
  faction).
- **Lineage pool.** The weaker's `lineagePool` is added to the
  stronger's, with overflow cascading per §4.6.
- **In-flight convoys.** Active convoys on the weaker lineage are
  reassigned to the stronger per §4.5.8 (source/dest ids remapped if
  their locations transferred; otherwise disband per the
  destination-died rule).
- **Empress.** If both lineages had empresses, the weaker's empress
  is killed outright on merge — the sole-empress invariant (§7.3.6)
  requires exactly one empress per lineage. If only the weaker had
  one, she's demoted back to a queen entity at her current position
  (the inverse of §8.4's promotion ritual, but instantaneous —
  there's no need for a public ceremony when she's just being
  reabsorbed).
- **Removal.** The weaker lineage faction is removed via BLib
  `Alien.MOD.factions().remove(weakerId)` with
  `LineageRemovalReason.AbsorbedBy(strongerId)` recorded for
  forensics.

#### 8.8.4 Trigger task

A periodic per-lineage task (`AbsorptionEligibilityTask`) checks
each pair of same-variant same-dimension lineages with adjacent
territory once per minute. The task tracks per-pair "first adjacent
tick" timestamps in the registry so the 5-minute threshold can be
evaluated cheaply. When the threshold is met and both lineages are
still non-angry, the merge fires.

If either lineage becomes angry mid-cooldown, the timer resets — a
player engaging either side blocks the merge until peace returns.

## 9. Validity & sanity rules

These are global predicates that maintain consistency:

| Rule                                                                                                  | Enforced by                          |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Variant faction has exactly one variant, set at creation, never changed                                | `VariantFactionData` constructor     |
| Lineage faction has exactly one variant, matching its parent variant faction                          | `LineageFactionData` constructor     |
| All lineage members have variant matching the lineage's variant                                        | `onMemberAdded` validates           |
| Every alien of variant V is a member of variant V's variant faction                                    | Alien tick adds membership lazily    |
| Lineage with `locationIds.size() >= 2` has a non-null `empressId` resolving to a living empress entity | `LineageFactionData.tick`            |
| Each lineage has at most one empress (sole-empress invariant)                                          | Empress entity creation validates    |
| `byChunk` map is one-to-one with `HiveLocation.claimedChunks`                                         | invariant; CRC checked at load       |
| Every location's `lineageFactionId` resolves to an existing lineage faction                            | invariant; orphan locations are scrubbed at startup |
| Every chunk in a location's `claimedChunks` is claimed by the location's lineage in BLib              | invariant; sync at startup           |
| A chunk in `byChunk[level][pos]` is claimed by exactly one location of any given lineage              | enforced by `addClaim`               |
| Two locations of the same variant never overlap chunks (any lineage)                                   | enforced by §5.1 step 3              |
| Variant pool counts are non-negative                                                                   | `EntityReserves` already does this   |
| Lineage pool counts are non-negative                                                                   | `EntityReserves` already does this   |

A `HiveLocationRegistry.validate()` runs at server start and logs (not
crashes) any inconsistency, attempting repair where possible.

## 11. Reserve / pool flow at a glance

```
                            ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                            │  Variant Pool (per dim)       │
                            │  (future Queen Mother source) │
                            └──────────────────────────────┘
                                           ▲
                                           │  §8.7  lineage death overflow
                                           │
                            ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                            │  Lineage Pool (per lineage)  │
                            └──────────────────────────────┘
                              │             ▲
       §4.5.7 in-place pool   │             │  §4.4 shedding
       reinforcement          │             │  (lineage ↑ 1, alien despawns)
       (EMPRESS GATED)        │             │
       (no transit)           │             │  §4.5.8 abstract convoy disband
                              ▼             │  (destination died, expired) — refund
                  ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │  Hive Location reserves (per location)  │
                  │     ▲                                   │
                  │     │ Reinforcement / Migration arrival  │
                  │     │ (drain composition into reserves)  │
                  └─────┴──────────────────────────────────┘
                    ▲          │
                    │  spawn   │  spawn (reserves ↓ 1, world entity ↑ 1)
       §4.2 sources │          │
                    │          │   §4.5.4 / §4.5.5 / §4.5.6 dispatch
                    │          │   (reserves ↓ N, drain into convoy)
                    │          ▼
                    │   ┌────────────────────────────────┐
                    │   │  Convoy (in flight)              │
                    │   │   • Reinforcement (sister surplus)│
                    │   │   • Migration (whole evacuation)  │
                    │   │   • Raid (chase player)           │
                    │   │  abstract or manifested            │
                    │   └────────────────────────────────┘
                    │          │
                    │          │   §4.5.3 manifest while in loaded chunks
                    │          ▼
                    │   ┌────────────────────────────────┐
                    │   │   World — loaded aliens          │ ◄── players can engage
                    │   └────────────────────────────────┘   (composition ↓ 1 per kill,
                    │                                         no refund — lost outright)
                    │
                    │  spawn (reserves ↓ 1, world entity ↑ 1)
                    ▼
            ┌──────────────────────────┐
            │   World — loaded aliens   │
            └──────────────────────────┘
                       │
            §4.6 spillover (overflow → next tier up)
                       ▼
            ┌──────────────────────────────┐
            │  Lineage Pool                 │
            └──────────────────────────────┘
```

Every transition is a single +N/-N — no magic. The flow is:

- **World ↔ local reserves** is always free (kills, despawns, ambient spawns).
- **Local reserves → convoy → other location's reserves**: the canonical
  cross-location path. Empress-gated, takes time, interceptable.
  Composition lost in transit is *gone forever* (no refund) unless the
  destination dies, in which case abstract-convoy refunds to the pool.
- **Local reserves ↔ lineage pool** is free for spillover (always
  flows up); the **in-place** path (pool → location, §4.5.6) is
  empress-gated, instantaneous, and is the fallback when no sister
  location can convoy.
- **Lineage pool ↔ variant pool** is one-way: lineage death pushes
  pool overflow to variant. Variant pool drains nowhere in v1 — it's
  reserved for the future Queen Mother.

## 14. Open questions

These are decisions worth resolving *before* code lands.

1. ~~**Variant scope of factions.**~~ **Resolved.** Three-tier
   design: one variant faction per variant (global), one lineage
   faction per bloodline (per dimension), one hive location per
   geographic anchor (within a lineage).

2. ~~**Cross-dimension factions / lineages.**~~ **Resolved.**
   - **Variant factions are cross-dimensional** (one per variant
     globally; at most 4 ever exist).
   - **Lineage factions are dimension-local.** A queen who travels
     through a portal and settles in a new dimension founds a new
     lineage there, parented to the same variant faction but
     otherwise independent of her old-dimension lineage. Cross-
     dimension lineage extension is not supported. (Convoys
     consequently never cross dimensions either — see §4.5.6.)

   Rationale: a lineage represents a geographically contiguous
   bloodline — locations spread outward by physical proximity
   (§5.7), members convoy between locations through real space
   (§4.5), and the empress's coordination is mediated by all of
   that being in one world. Dimensions are an inherently
   disconnected thing: there is no straight-line distance between
   the Overworld and the Nether, no contiguous chunk graph, no
   way to convoy across. Treating a portal trip as a *new lineage
   beginning* matches what's actually happening physically — the
   queen has left her bloodline's reach and is starting fresh.
   Variant identity persists across dimensions (a Nether-variant
   alien wandering the Overworld is still Nether-variant), but
   the lineage doesn't follow her through.

3. ~~**Forager attachment policy.**~~ **Resolved.** Foragers
   re-join a lineage by physical presence: entering a same-variant
   lineage's claimed territory and staying for
   `FORAGER_JOIN_TICKS` (default 30 seconds). No explicit
   `requestToJoin` ceremony — the join is automatic. This matches
   the player-visible behavior ("xenomorph wandered into the
   nest, became part of it") and is symmetrical with shedding,
   which is also distance-driven.

4. **Variant Pool decay.** Should pooled counts decay over time?
   *Recommendation: yes, at a slow rate (e.g. 1% per real-world
   day) to prevent infinite buildup on idle worlds. Pool is a
   buffer, not a savings account.*

5. ~~**Empress entity type.**~~ **Resolved: it already exists.**
   `com.alien.common.gameplay.entity.living.alien.xenomorph.empress.Empress`
   is registered as a real `EntityType` for all four variants
   (`EMPRESS`, `ABERRANT_EMPRESS`, `NETHER_EMPRESS`,
   `IRRADIATED_EMPRESS` in `AlienEntityTypes`), with its own
   `EmpressData`, `EmpressOvipositorManager`,
   `EmpressAnimationDispatcher`, and `EmpressGOAP`. The redesign
   reuses these existing types directly — no new entity work is
   needed for the empress role itself, only the lineage-tier
   leadership behavior wired around them.

6. ~~**Empress promotion mechanics.**~~ **Resolved: visible
   ritual.** The chosen queen undergoes a 30-second molt-style
   transition before her queen entity is replaced with the
   appropriate variant's empress entity. During the molt:
   - She is **invulnerable** to all damage (death during molt
     would invalidate emergence and is not allowed).
   - She is **immobile** — animation overrides movement.
   - She is **persistence-required** — chunk unload is suppressed
     for her duration.
   - A visible animation plays (reuse the molting visual pipeline
     where possible — `MoltingManager` already exists).
   - At molt end, her queen entity is discarded and replaced with
     the empress entity (per variant — `EMPRESS`,
     `ABERRANT_EMPRESS`, `NETHER_EMPRESS`, or `IRRADIATED_EMPRESS`)
     at her position. Stats and lineage membership transfer.
   This gives players a clear, telegraphed event ("a queen is
   becoming an empress") rather than an instantaneous swap.

7. ~~**Civil war pool partition.**~~ **Resolved: equal split.**
   The shattered lineage's pool divides evenly across successor
   lineages, with remainder going to the successor whose seat is
   the oldest location (per the existing §8.5 rule). Weighted
   partition (by location size, territory, or member count) is
   parked as a possible v2 refinement if equal-split feels
   unfair in playtests — but for v1 the simplicity is more
   valuable than the realism.

8. ~~**Lineage absorption on/off.**~~ **Resolved: keep,
   strictly gated.** Same-variant lineages may absorb each other
   per §8.8, but the gating is tighter than today's
   `MergeWithNearbyHiveTask`: claim-adjacency (not radius
   overlap), both non-angry, and continuously adjacent for >5
   minutes before the merge fires. The mechanic remains so that
   AI-driven lineages that grow into each other naturally
   consolidate rather than burning eternally in border
   skirmishes.

9. ~~**Player factions.**~~ **Resolved: not for v1.** Players
   cannot join hive factions in the initial implementation. Parked
   as a future possibility — most likely surfacing alongside any
   future *playable xenomorph* mechanics, where a player-controlled
   xenomorph would naturally need lineage / variant faction
   membership to interact with the rest of the hive system. The
   current design doesn't preclude it (BLib supports player-faction
   membership), so the door stays open.

10. ~~**Migration from existing saves.**~~ **Resolved: explicit
    migration, no hard reset.** Each old `Hive` becomes one
    `HiveLocation` of a freshly-minted single-location
    `LineageFaction`. The mapping:

    - **Variant Faction:** look up (or lazily create) the variant
      faction for the old hive's variant.
    - **Lineage Faction:** mint one fresh `LineageFaction` per old
      hive, in the old hive's dimension, parented to the
      appropriate variant faction. The lineage's `founderId` is
      copied from the old hive's leader UUID if present, else from
      any queen in the old hive's membership, else null. No
      empress is created — each migrated lineage is single-location
      and doesn't need one.
    - **Hive Location:** the old hive's `centerPos` becomes the new
      location's fixed `centerPos` (it stops moving). Initial
      claimed chunks = every chunk that fell inside the old hive's
      sphere radius (`AlienProperties.Hive.RADIUS_IN_BLOCKS`,
      default 64). These claims are filed under the new lineage's
      faction id via BLib `addClaim`.
    - **Reserves:** the old hive's `HiveReserveManager` reserves
      are copied directly into the new location's
      `HiveLocationReserves`. The lineage pool starts empty.
    - **Members:** the old hive's faction membership is migrated to
      the new lineage (BLib faction transfer). Each member also
      lazily joins the variant faction on next load.
    - **Boss bar:** dropped at migration; reconstructed by the new
      location on its first tick.

    Migration runs once per server start when an old-format `Hive`
    is encountered; the old `HiveRegistry`/`HiveFactionData` is
    decommissioned afterwards. A migration log records the
    old-id → new-(lineageId, locationId) mapping for forensics.

11. ~~**Multi-empress rituals.**~~ **Resolved.** Use the
    deterministic tiebreak from §8.4 for v1 (older queen → queen
    at older location → founder). Combat-based selection (eligible
    queens fight, winner becomes empress) is parked as a possible
    future feature — interesting flavor, but not required for the
    initial system.

12. ~~**What happens if the empress wanders out of all of her
    lineage's territory?**~~ **Resolved.** The empress is exempt
    from shedding entirely — she will not be removed from her
    lineage by distance, and her wandering does not trigger civil
    war. If a player leads her hundreds of blocks away, she
    simply keeps going where led; she remains alive and is still
    the lineage's empress. All convoy-dispatch logic (§4.5.4–
    §4.5.6) and in-place pool reinforcement (§4.5.7) continue to
    work for any of her lineage's locations as long as she is
    alive — the empress's location and the requesting location do
    not have to be co-located. The only event that breaks the
    lineage is her *death* (§8.5).

13. ~~**Convoy pathing.**~~ **Resolved: straight-line for v1**
    (per §4.5.2). Convoys clip abstractly through unloaded chunks
    and only interact with the world when manifested. Routing
    convoys through a graph of claimed territory + lineage spread
    zones ("marching along the resin highways") is parked as a
    revisit candidate — to be reconsidered if straight-line travel
    produces awkward "convoy phases through a mountain" moments
    that hurt immersion when manifestation happens at a weird
    spot.

14. ~~**Convoy speed scaling with composition.**~~ **Resolved.**
    Convoys move at the speed of their slowest unit. Per-type
    speed contributions are configured per `EntityType<?>`
    (drones / runners contribute high, warriors / prowlers
    moderate, praetorians / crushers / queens / empresses low).
    The default `CONVOY_SPEED_BLOCKS_PER_SECOND` is used as the
    speed contribution for any type without a per-type override.
    Per-convoy-type multipliers (reinforcement 1.0× / migration
    0.7× / raid 1.5×) compose on top of the slowest-unit speed.
    Speed is recomputed when composition changes (e.g. members
    die during manifestation) — a convoy whose slow members get
    killed off speeds up for the rest of its journey.

15. ~~**Player-faction relations and convoys.**~~ **Resolved: out
    of scope for v1.** Convoys are simply AVP-faction members who
    follow normal hostility rules — no special interaction with a
    player's BLib faction relationships against the lineage. If
    cross-faction diplomacy ever lands as a feature (e.g.
    AVP-Predator alliances with a specific lineage), this question
    should be revisited.

16. ~~**Raid attribution accuracy.**~~ **Resolved.** Raid
    attribution defers to Minecraft's standard kill-credit rules.
    A lineage-member death is counted against a player iff vanilla
    would attribute the kill to that player — i.e. the alien's
    `getKillCredit()` resolves to that `Player` (or
    `lastHurtByPlayer` is the player and the death happened within
    vanilla's attribution window). Indirect kills count exactly as
    vanilla counts them: a creeper that the player aggro'd into a
    drone counts; fall damage from a player-broken support counts;
    fire from a player-placed source counts; an alien killed by an
    unrelated zombie does not. We don't roll our own attribution
    logic — if Minecraft considers it a player kill, the lineage
    does too.

17. ~~**Multi-player raids.**~~ **Resolved.** At most one active
    raid convoy per source location at any given time, targeting
    the player with the highest recent kill count against the
    lineage. Multiple source locations within the same lineage may
    each dispatch their own raid concurrently — potentially at the
    same player, or at different players.

18. ~~**Migration eligibility for the destination.**~~ **Resolved.**
    When multiple sister locations are eligible, the migration
    destination is the closest sister by straight-line distance from
    the source's center. Ties are broken by largest claimed-chunk
    count (migrate to the strongest nearby foothold).

19. ~~**Evacuating-state visibility.**~~ **Resolved.** The
    Evacuating state is player-visible via the location's boss
    bar:
    - Bar color shifts to amber (`BossBarColor.YELLOW`).
    - Bar title is suffixed with " (Evacuating)".
    - The shift is held for the full `MIGRATION_RALLY_TICKS`
      window (default 30 seconds) and persists until the convoy
      dispatches and the location dies.
    A player who has been softening a hive sees the visual
    confirmation that their pressure broke the location, and
    sees a short window in which to interfere with the
    evacuation rally before the convoy departs.

20. ~~**Raid de-escalation.**~~ **Resolved: no de-escalation.**
    Once a raid is dispatched it runs to one of its termination
    conditions in §4.5.6. There is no path by which the lineage
    forgives the player or recalls the convoy because the player
    stopped attacking — xenomorphs have no forgiveness or mercy.
    The 30-minute expiry is the only natural deadline; everything
    else is "target dead, source dead, or all of us dead."

## 15. Implementation outline (high-level)

A rough order of work, with no estimates.

1. **`HiveLocation` and registry** — types, persistence, indexes.
   No behavior yet, just the data plumbing.
2. **`VariantFactionData`** — new BLib `FactionDataType`. Lazy
   creation hook in `Alien.tick` so every alien lands in their
   variant faction.
3. **`LineageFactionData` rewrite** — replaces today's
   `HiveFactionData`. Variant lock, location set, empress field,
   pool, removal-reason sealed interface, validation in
   `onMemberAdded`.
4. **`HiveLocationRegistry.tick`** — replaces `HiveRegistry.tick`.
   Per-location `tick()` calls. Iterates across all levels (fixes
   the overworld-only bug).
5. **Boss bar** — `HiveLocationBossBar` with distance-radius
   visibility (§6).
6. **Per-location leadership** — `HiveLocationLeadership` per
   location, scoped to xenomorphs in claimed chunks.
7. **Local reserves** — port `HiveReserveManager` to per-location.
8. **Lineage pool** — embedded in `LineageFactionData`. Shedding
   hook in `Alien.tick`. Empress-gated *in-place* pool
   reinforcement (§4.5.6) in `CryForHelpListener` and natural
   spawn paths.
8a. **Convoy machinery (common)** — `Convoy` sealed interface and
    its three records (Reinforcement, Migration, Raid). Persistence
    on `LineageFactionData.convoys`. Per-tick travel advancement
    (lerp toward target, ignores chunk-load state). Manifestation
    logic: when `currentPos` lands in chunks loaded by a player
    within `MANIFEST_DISTANCE_BLOCKS`, spawn composition as real
    entities; despawn on chunk unload while updating `currentPos`
    from the entity centroid (§4.5.2, §4.5.3).
8b. **Reinforcement convoys** — dispatch via cry-for-help relay
    and empress-tick surplus rebalance. "In transit" AI goal,
    non-aggressive. Arrival drains into destination reserves
    (§4.5.4).
8c. **Migration convoys** — trigger detection (queen-loss /
    biomass-decay / territory-floor). `Evacuating` state on source
    with rally period. Drain reserves + despawn xenomorphs into
    composition. Carry empress if she's at source. Source
    decommissioned on dispatch. Arrival adds biomass payload and
    respawns empress (§4.5.5).
8d. **Raid convoys** — kill-attribution tracking on the lineage
    (player UUIDs + recent kill counts). Trigger when threshold
    met and source size sufficient. Per-tick destination update
    from target player position. "Engage target" AI goal —
    aggressive, breaks formation. Termination conditions: target
    death / target offline / expiry / source death (§4.5.6).
8e. **Convoy failure handling** — destination death, source death,
    empress death, civil war partition, lineage absorption,
    manifested wipeout, raid offline-conversion (§4.5.8).
9. **Variant pool** — overflow target only for v1 (no Queen
   Mother yet). Per-dim, per-variant.
10. **Territory expansion** — biomass tracking, claim cost curve,
    activity-gated claim attempts as a new `HiveLocationTask`.
11. **Caste distance rule** — replaces `HiveSpaceManager` in
    `AlienSpawning`, `EggLayingSensors`, `ResinManager`,
    `QueenSpawning`, `ResinVentBlockEntity`, etc.
12. **Lineage spread spawning** — new task on `LineageFactionData`
    that pulls from the lineage pool to spawn shed members back
    into the world within the spread zone (empress-gated).
13. **Contest resolution** — new task scanning
    `getAllContestedChunks` for chunks involving our factions.
14. **Founding** — queen settlement detection, location founding,
    lineage founding when no same-lineage container exists.
15. **Empress disposition wiring** — the `Empress` entity type and
    its variants already exist (`EMPRESS`, `ABERRANT_EMPRESS`,
    `NETHER_EMPRESS`, `IRRADIATED_EMPRESS`). This step is just
    extending `HiveLeaderDispositionUtil` to put them at rank 5
    (above queens at 4) and ensuring the `EMPRESSES` tag covers
    all four variants.
16. **Empress emergence** — auto-promotion of strongest queen in
    a lineage when it first owns 2+ locations.
17. **Empress death / civil war** — partition members, mint new
    lineages, transfer claims, fire advancement.
18. **Death paths** — location dormancy, location death, lineage
    death.
19. **Migration** — one-shot converter for old hive data on first
    load of a new-system world (see §14 #10 for the full mapping):
    each old `Hive` becomes a single-location `LineageFaction`
    parented to the matching variant faction. Old `centerPos`
    becomes the new (fixed) location center; chunks within the old
    sphere radius are claimed via BLib `addClaim` under the new
    lineage; old reserves copy into the new location's local
    reserves; old membership migrates to the new lineage and
    lazily joins the variant faction. No empress is created (each
    migrated lineage starts single-location). Migration log
    records old-id → new-(lineageId, locationId) mappings.
20. **Cleanup** — delete `HiveSpaceManager`, `MergeWithNearbyHiveTask`,
    `Hive`, `HiveRegistry`, all the moving-center logic. Keep
    `HiveIds` (rename to `HiveLocationIds` and add lineage / variant
    id helpers), `HiveLeaderDispositionUtil` (extend with empress
    rank), `HiveVentManager`, `HiveBossBarManager` (refactored).

Each step is separately mergeable as long as the old system still
runs alongside (feature flag the new one). Hard cutover happens at
step 20.

## 16. Summary

A hive in this redesign is a **three-tier hierarchy**:

- The **Variant Faction** (one per variant, global) holds species-wide
  membership and reserves a slot for the future Queen Mother.
- A **Lineage Faction** (per bloodline, per dimension) holds a queen's
  bloodline, owns one or more hive locations, and — when it owns more
  than one — has an **Empress** as its lineage-wide leader.
- A **Hive Location** (fixed point) anchors territory, reserves,
  leadership, and a boss bar. Locations are minted by queens settling,
  and a lineage geographically expands one location at a time within
  a `MAX_LINEAGE_SPREAD_CHUNKS` zone of its existing locations.

Growth is paid for in **biomass**, generated by hive activity inside
claimed chunks and consumed by claim attempts whose cost grows
super-linearly with hive size. The hive can grow without bound *if* it
can sustain biomass production proportional to its territory; if it
can't, it shrinks at the edges first.

Population scales beyond Minecraft's tickable-entity limit through a
three-tier reserve system: **per-location reserves** for that
location's spawning needs, a **per-lineage Lineage Pool** that holds
shed members and feeds reinforcements back, and a **per-dimension
Variant Pool** that catches lineage-pool overflow when lineages die
(reserved for future Queen Mother mechanics). Aliens that wander too
far from their lineage's territory are slowly shed into the lineage
pool and despawned; locations under attack pull from the pool to
summon reinforcements — but **only when an empress is alive**. Without
her, hive locations are reduced to their local reserves only and the
lineage cannot coordinate.

If the empress is killed, the lineage **shatters into N
single-location lineages** (one per former location), each with its
own share of the shattered lineage's pool. This is the design's
explicit "civil war" mechanic — a single point of failure that is
also a single point of high-value player target.

Boss bars belong to **locations**, are visible by **distance from the
location's center**, and count only xenomorphs in **that** location's
territory. The leader of each location is picked from xenomorphs
physically present in its territory, satisfying "always have a leader"
as long as the location isn't dormant.

Most of the old system's flaws are fixed structurally rather than
patched: the moving center disappears, the hive-with-eggs-dies bug
disappears, the spatial model becomes the same chunk-claim system the
rest of BLib speaks, and the linear scans become indexed lookups.
